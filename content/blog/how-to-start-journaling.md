## How do I start journaling?

Starting a journaling habit is simpler than most people think. You do not need a perfect routine, a leather-bound notebook, or a quiet hour. You just need a few minutes and a way to record your thoughts — whether that is an app, a notebook, or a notes file.

The most important rule: there are no rules. Journaling works when you do it consistently, not when you do it perfectly.

## What should I write about in my journal?

Anything that is on your mind. Here are approaches that work for beginners:

- **What happened today.** A brief summary of the day's events and how you felt about them.
- **How you are feeling right now.** Name the emotion and describe it without judging yourself.
- **A problem you are working through.** Write out both sides and what you are leaning toward.
- **Something you are grateful for.** One specific thing and why it mattered today.
- **A prompt.** If you are stuck, use a prompt like "What is occupying my mind today?" or "What am I avoiding?"

## How often should I journal?

Start with once or twice per week. Consistency matters more than volume. A 3-minute voice entry three times a week is more valuable than an hour-long session once a month. As journaling becomes a habit, increase the frequency if you want to.

## How long should a journal entry be?

A few sentences is fine. There is no minimum length. A short entry is better than no entry. Over time, you may naturally write more on days when you have more to process.

## Common beginner obstacles and how to overcome them

- **"I do not know what to write."** Use a guided prompt. Elora suggests prompts based on patterns from your own entries. Or start by describing what you did today and how you felt.
- **"I do not have time."** Voice journaling takes less than 2 minutes. Speak your entry while making coffee or commuting.
- **"It feels awkward."** The first few entries feel unusual. This goes away within a week or two of regular practice.
- **"I worry about someone reading it."** Use an end-to-end encrypted journaling app like Elora so only you can read your entries.
